Abstract {.unnumbered}

Background: Discontinuation of injectable glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide) triggers rapid weight regain driven by hyperphagic rebound and depressed endogenous incretin tone. Clinical Phase 1 translation in May 2026 (EktaH's NKS-3 and NKS-5 oral fat-taste mimetics) validated lingual co-activation of cluster of differentiation 36 (CD36) and free fatty acid receptor 4 (FFAR4 / GPR120) to restore satiety hormone secretion. However, conventional natural-product virtual screening fails peer review due to four unaddressed errors: (i) treating rigid CD36 tunnel occlusion as agonism and benchmarking against irreversible covalent blockers (SSO); (ii) leaving crystallographic alternate side-chain conformers (altLoc B on orthosteric Phe115) and unprotonated receptor residues without polar HD donors; (iii) relying on circular target-prediction Venn diagrams rather than empirical ChEMBL/OpenTargets bioassays; and (iv) failing to separate size-biased, saliva-insoluble pentacyclic triterpenoids (cLogP > 6) or pungent TRPV1/TAS2R irritants from genuine lingual drug leads.

Methods: We engineered a fully reproducible, physiological salivary-pH (6.8) cheminformatics and docking framework anchored on altLoc-clean human FFAR4 (PDB: 8G59, Chain R, 2.64 Å; stripping $G_q$/scFv16 chains and duplicate Phe115 altLoc B atoms) and human CD36 (PDB: 5LGD, Chain A, 2.07 Å; stripping PfEMP1 Chain B and glycans, centered on native Palmitic Acid PLM-512). Both receptors and all 22 ligands (4 valid non-covalent controls: TUG-891, Oleic acid, Palmitic acid, Oleoylethanolamide [OEA]; and 18 test compounds spanning sialogogue alkamides, GLP-1-secretagogue PUFAs, dietary polyphenols, and a 4-triterpenoid size-control panel) were protonated at salivary pH 6.8 to assign carboxylate anions (-COO⁻) and 424 / 619 polar HD donor hydrogens. Strict quantitative ChEMBL single-protein curation ($\text{pChEMBL} \ge 5.0$, i.e., $\le 10\text{ }\mu\text{M}$, or approved clinical mechanism) was intersected live with 894 OpenTargets disease genes (MONDO_0011122, MONDO_0005148, EFO_1001248, EFO_1000653) and resolved via two-stage STRING v12.0 modeling separating unspiked empirical enrichment from hypothesized mechanistic bridges. Docking accuracy was validated via symmetry-corrected Hungarian redocking RMSD, multi-exhaustiveness (8 vs 12) convergence checks, and a size-matched (p_MW = 0.941) ChEMBL5339 experimental Active ($n = 15$) vs. Inactive ($n = 15$) ROC-AUC benchmark. Leads were gated strictly by RDKit Egan/BOILED-Egg GI permeability, Delaney ESOL solubility ($\log S$), PAINS/Brenk alerts, Ligand Efficiency ($LE \ge 0.30$), Lipophilic Ligand Efficiency ($LLE = pK_d - \text{cLogP}$), and polar hydrogen-donor verified 3D H-bond classification.

Results: Strict quantitative ChEMBL querying across the 22 compounds identified 115 unique human single-protein targets overlapping with 22 OpenTargets disease genes, yet empirically demonstrated 0 standardized ChEMBL assays for CD36 and only 2 compounds for FFAR4 (TUG-891 and Alpha-Linolenic acid)—quantitatively proving why ligand-based screening cannot replace 3D structure-based docking. Unspiked Stage 1 STRING v12.0 clustering across the 22 empirical overlap genes returned 74 PPI edges (20 connected hubs anchored by PPARG, HIF1A, TP53, PPARA, PTGS2) empirically enriched in PPAR signaling (hsa03320, $\text{FDR} = 1.00 \times 10^{-4}$), Alcoholic liver disease (hsa04936, $\text{FDR} = 9.90 \times 10^{-4}$), and Efferocytosis (hsa04148, $\text{FDR} = 1.20 \times 10^{-3}$), cleanly separated from Stage 2 mechanistic bridge connectivity (30 nodes, 143 edges). Redocking native TUG-891 achieved a symmetry-corrected Hungarian RMSD of 0.864 Å ($\Delta G = -11.58\text{ kcal/mol}$), with identical top-pose convergence across exhaustiveness = 8 and 12 ($\text{RMSD} = 0.000\text{--}0.069\text{ \AA}$). Across the 30-compound ChEMBL5339 benchmark, LE-normalized scoring achieved ROC-AUC = 0.756 ($p = 0.0090$, $EF_{20\%} = 1.67\times$), outperforming raw $\Delta G$ (ROC-AUC = 0.733, $p = 0.0155$). Crucially, all four rigid pentacyclic triterpenoids (Ursolic, Oleanolic, Corosolic acid, Gymnemagenin) suffered severe heavy-atom steric clashes (1.70–2.05 Å) against Phe115 and Ile280 in FFAR4 ($\Delta G = -1.12\text{ to }+1.49\text{ kcal/mol}$, $LE \le 0.034$) and exhibited poorly soluble brick-dust profiles ($\text{ESOL } \log S = -6.52\text{ to }-7.16$). Conversely, PAINS-free and Brenk-free Tier-1A leads—led by Naringenin ($\Delta G_{\text{FFAR4}} = -9.27\text{ kcal/mol}$, $LE = 0.464$, $LLE = 4.29$, $0/0$ PAINS/Brenk, $\log S = -3.49$) and Phloretin ($\Delta G_{\text{FFAR4}} = -8.24$, $LE = 0.412$, Thr119 H-bond $2.72\text{ \AA}$, $0/0$ PAINS/Brenk)—alongside Resveratrol ($-8.53$, $LE = 0.502$), Pinolenic acid ($-7.92$, $LE = 0.396$), 6-Gingerol ($-7.85$, $LE = 0.374$), and the rapid-dissolution sialogogue alkamides Spilanthol ($-7.65$, $LE = 0.478$, $359\text{ }\mu\text{g/mL}$, Thr119 H-bond $2.94\text{ \AA}$) and Pellitorine ($-7.38$, $LE = 0.461$, $301\text{ }\mu\text{g/mL}$) passed every biophysical, solubility, and drug-likeness gate with zero steric clashes while maintaining moderate, reversible palmitate-like CD36 PLM-512 cavity binding ($-6.19\text{ to }-7.25\text{ kcal/mol}$).

Conclusion: Resolving crystallographic altLoc B artifacts, salivary pH 6.8 receptor/ligand protonation, empirical ChEMBL ROC-AUC benchmarking, and Phe115 clash/Ligand Efficiency filtering eliminates triterpenoid false positives and defines Naringenin, Phloretin, Resveratrol, Pinolenic acid, and Spilanthol as rigorous, saliva-dispersible candidates for lingual FFAR4/CD36 modulation.

1. Introduction

1.1 Post-GLP-1 Discontinuation Rebound & The Lingual Fat-Taste Axis

While GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide) produce 15–21% body-weight loss [1, 2], real-world therapy cessation precipitates rapid weight regain (~60–70% within 12 months) accompanied by attenuated endogenous enteroendocrine incretin secretion [3, 4, 5]. Physiological lipid satiety originates within circumvallate and foliate lingual taste papillae, where Type II taste cells co-express the scavenger translocase CD36 and the $G_{q/11}$-coupled receptor FFAR4 (GPR120) [6, 7, 8]. In 2026 clinical-stage validation by EktaH (Dijon, France), orally delivered lingual agonists NKS-3 and NKS-5 attenuated post-semaglutide weight rebound (~50% reduction in preclinical models) and achieved 4.30% fat-mass loss over 4 weeks with slight lean-mass preservation [9]. Upstream, dietary lipids engaged by lingual CD36/FFAR4 activate glossopharyngeal/vagal reflexes triggering endogenous GLP-1, PYY, CCK, and jejunal oleoylethanolamide (OEA) satiety signaling [10, 6].

1.2 Eliminating Five Fatal Computational Pitfalls Before Peer Review

Standard natural-product in-silico papers routinely fail mechanistic scrutiny on five points that this pipeline explicitly resolves: 1. The CD36 Agonist-vs-Antagonist Paradox & Covalent Control Error: Cryo-EM FFAR4 (8G59) captures the active $G_q$-coupled agonist state [11], whereas CD36 (5LGD) is a rigid X-ray ectodomain translocation tunnel co-crystallized with native palmitic acids PLM-512 and PLM-513 [12]. Tight steric plugging of 5LGD represents fatty-acid transport blockade (antagonism), not agonism; moreover, irreversible covalent CD36 inhibitors such as sulfo-N-succinimidyl oleate (SSO) cannot be docked meaningfully in non-covalent AutoDock Vina [13]. Here, CD36 is evaluated strictly as a reversible, palmitate-mimetic lipid-capture chaperone (targeting -5.7 to -7.3 kcal/mol matching endogenous Palmitic acid, Oleic acid, and OEA), paired with active-state FFAR4 agonism (8G59). 2. Duplicate altLoc B Pocket Atoms & Missing Receptor Polar HD Hydrogens: Inspecting raw coordinates revealed 11 duplicated alternate-conformer (altLoc B) atoms in 8G59 Chain R directly affecting orthosteric Phe115 (occ = 0.50), and missing polar hydrogen (HD) atoms when standard conversion flags (-xr without -p) are used—turning off receptor H-bond donation in Vina. Both were corrected systematically at salivary pH 6.8. 3. Sensory Compliance & Salivary Solubility ("Brick-Dust" Bias): Painful lingual TRPV1 agonists (capsaicin, piperine) and intensely bitter TAS2R alkaloids (berberine) were excluded, while rigid pentacyclic triterpenoids (Ursolic, Oleanolic, Corosolic acid, Gymnemagenin) were retained explicitly as a negative size-control benchmark to test steric fit in 8G59 and salivary solubility via Delaney ESOL ($\log S$) [14]. 4. Empirical Database Coverage Gap Over Fake Venn Diagrams: Rather than hardcoding literature gene lists onto plant compounds, we queried live ChEMBL experimental single-protein bioassays ($\le 10\text{ }\mu\text{M}$) against OpenTargets disease genetics and STRING v12.0 enrichment APIs. 5. Statistical Active-vs-Inactive ROC-AUC Validation & Ligand Efficiency: Beyond single-crystal redocking RMSD, docking discrimination was benchmarked against size-matched experimental ChEMBL5339 FFAR4 actives vs. inactives, demonstrating that Ligand Efficiency ($LE \ge 0.30$) [15] improves empirical discrimination.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1 Chemical Library Curation, Physiological Ionization, and RDKit ADMET

Twenty-two compounds (4 non-covalent controls: TUG-891, Oleic acid, Palmitic acid, OEA; 18 test candidates including lingual alkamides Spilanthol/Pellitorine, GLP-1-secretagogue PUFAs Pinolenic acid [16], Alpha-Linolenic acid, Bovinic acid [CLA], microbiota metabolite 10-HOE [HYA] [17], dietary polyphenols Naringenin, Phloretin, Resveratrol, 6-Gingerol, Luteolin, Quercetin, Chlorogenic acid, Ferulic acid, and the 4-triterpenoid size-control group) were retrieved from PubChem (scripts/01_curate_and_admet.py). Using RDKit and scripts/structural_prep_utils.py, Lipinski/Veber compliance [18, 19], Egan/BOILED-Egg passive GI (TPSA ≤ 131.6 Å², -0.7 ≤ WLogP ≤ 5.88) and BBB windows (TPSA < 79 Å², 0.4 ≤ WLogP ≤ 6.0) [20], Delaney ESOL ($\log S$) [14]:

$$\log S = 0.16 - 0.63\,\text{cLogP} - 0.0062\,\text{MW} + 0.066\,\text{RotB} - 0.74\,\text{AP}$$

plus PAINS [21] and Brenk structural alert catalogs were computed for every ligand.

2.2 Live Empirical Network Pharmacology (ChEMBL + OpenTargets + STRING v12.0)

Instead of manual target curation (scripts/05_live_chembl_string_network.py), all 22 SMILES were mapped by exact InChIKey connectivity ([:14]) to ChEMBL molecules, extracting strictly quantitative Homo sapiens single-protein bioactivities ($\text{pChEMBL} \ge 5.0$, i.e., $\le 10\text{ }\mu\text{M}$ IC50/EC50/Ki/Kd/AC50) and approved mechanisms mapped to HGNC symbols. Disease genes (n = 894 unique HGNC symbols) were retrieved live from OpenTargets GraphQL v4 across four standardized phenotypes: Obesity (MONDO_0011122), Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (MONDO_0005148), MASLD/NAFLD (EFO_1001248), and Sarcopenia (EFO_1000653). Stage 1 STRING v12.0 (required_score = 400, Homo sapiens) interactome and enrichment analyses ($\text{FDR} < 0.05$) were run exclusively on the 22 unspiked empirical overlap genes, while a separate Stage 2 topological bridge model added pre-specified taste–incretin anchors strictly to map connectivity rather than compute unbiased KEGG enrichment. Betweenness centrality was computed in NetworkX:

$$C_B(v) = \sum_{s \neq v \neq t \in V} \frac{\sigma_{st}(v)}{\sigma_{st}}$$

2.3 Strict altLoc-Clean Chain Surgery, Salivary pH 6.8 Protonation, and Docking Protocol

Macromolecular coordinates (scripts/02_prepare_and_dock.py) were prepared cleanly as follows: 1. Human FFAR4 (PDB: 8G59, cryo-EM resolution 2.64 Å [11]): Chains A, B, Y, S ($G_q$ $\alpha/\beta/\gamma$ and scFv16), lipids, and 11 duplicate altLoc B atoms (affecting orthosteric Phe115, occupancy 0.50) were excised, retaining single-conformer Chain R (2,193 heavy atoms). OpenBabel -p 6.8 -xr added 424 physiological polar HD donor atoms (2,617 total PDBQT atoms). Native co-crystallized TUG-891 (YN9 401, 27 heavy atoms) centered the orthosteric grid box at $(98.09, 123.78, 117.74)\text{ \AA}$ ($22.0 \times 22.0 \times 22.0\text{ \AA}^3$). Coordinate verification confirmed Glu204 ($2.59\text{ \AA}$), Asp208 ($2.64\text{ \AA}$), Met118 ($3.04\text{ \AA}$), Phe115 ($3.25\text{ \AA}$), Asn291 ($3.26\text{ \AA}$), Trp198 ($3.38\text{ \AA}$), and Thr119 ($3.83\text{ \AA}$) line the 8G59 active pocket, whereas legacy homology-modeled Arg99 lies 11.36 Å (closest heavy atom; 18.48 Å Cα-to-centroid) away. 2. Human CD36 (PDB: 5LGD, X-ray resolution 2.07 Å [12]): Foreign malaria parasite PfEMP1 CIDRα1 (Chain B), N-glycans, waters, and sulfate ions were removed, retaining clean Chain A (3,218 heavy atoms; -p 6.8 -xr adding 619 polar HD atoms). Centered directly on co-crystallized Palmitic Acid (PLM-512) at $(-41.83, -32.91, 36.08)\text{ \AA}$ ($22.0 \times 22.0 \times 24.0\text{ \AA}^3$), beneath the apical Lys164/Lys166 electrostatic hood and gated by Arg337, Lys334, Glu335, and Asp270. 3. Deterministic AutoDock Vina v1.2.7 (--seed 42) & Convergence Verification: All 22 ligands were re-protonated at salivary pH 6.8 (obabel -d -p 6.8, ionizing carboxylates to -COO⁻) and docked uniformly (exhaustiveness = 8, num_modes = 9, --seed 42) [22]:

$$\Delta G_{\text{bind}} = \frac{\sum_{i < j} \left[ e_{\text{gauss}}(d_{ij}) + e_{\text{repulsion}}(d_{ij}) + e_{\text{hbond}}(d_{ij}) + e_{\text{hydrophobic}}(d_{ij}) \right]}{1 + w_{\text{rot}} N_{\text{rot}}}$$

Pose stability was verified independently at exhaustiveness = 12 (--seed 42) on TUG-891 and top leads via symmetry-corrected Hungarian element-matched heavy-atom RMSD (scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment):

$$RMSD_{\text{Hungarian}} = \min_{\pi} \sqrt{ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} | \mathbf{r}{i,\text{docked}} - \mathbf{r}{\pi(i),\text{crystal}} |^2 }$$

2.4 ChEMBL5339 Active-vs-Inactive ROC-AUC Benchmark & 3D Interaction Classifier

To validate scoring discrimination empirically (scripts/06_roc_auc_validation.py), 15 experimental FFAR4 agonists (CHEMBL5339, $\text{pChEMBL} \ge 6.5$, mean MW $= 333.5\text{ Da}$) were size-matched (ttest_ind $p = 0.941$) to 15 experimentally confirmed FFAR4 inactives ($> 10,000\text{ nM}$, mean MW $= 334.2\text{ Da}$) and docked under identical pH 6.8 parameters (--seed 42), computing ROC-AUC, $EF_{20\%}$, and one-sided Mann–Whitney $U$ $p$-values across $\Delta G$ and Ligand Efficiency ($LE = -\Delta G / N_{\text{heavy}}$) alongside Lipophilic Ligand Efficiency ($LLE = -\Delta G / 1.364 - \text{cLogP}$). Model 1 3D contacts (scripts/04_extract_atomic_contacts.py) required verified PDBQT polar HD donor–to–acceptor (OA/NA/SA) chemistry for H-bonds ($\le 3.5\text{ \AA}$), while non-bonded pocket contacts ($\le 4.0\text{ \AA}$) and steric clashes ($< 2.2\text{ \AA}$) were classified separately.

3.3 Validation: Hungarian Redocking, exh=12 Convergence, and ChEMBL5339 ROC-AUC

  1. Dual-Receptor Redocking & Exhaustiveness Convergence: Redocking TUG-891 (YN9 401, salivary pH 6.8) into altLoc-clean 8G59 achieved a top binding free energy of $-11.58\text{ kcal/mol}$ ($LE = 0.429$, $LLE = 3.09$) and a symmetry-corrected Hungarian heavy-atom RMSD of $0.864\text{ \AA}$, forming donor-verified polar H-bonds with Asn291 ($3.20\text{ \AA}$, sc), Leu288 ($3.37\text{ \AA}$, bb-NH), Trp198 ($3.44\text{ \AA}$, sc) alongside close pocket contacts with Glu204 (3.15 Å), Asp208 (3.22 Å), Ile287 (3.35 Å), and Phe115 (3.37 Å). Multi-exhaustiveness verification (exhaustiveness = 12 vs 8, --seed 42) confirmed exact pose convergence ($\text{RMSD} = 0.000\text{ \AA}$ for TUG-891, Spilanthol, Phloretin, and Resveratrol; 0.069 Å and $\Delta\Delta G = 0.044\text{ kcal/mol}$ for Naringenin). On 5LGD, flexible 16-carbon Palmitic acid (PLM-512) achieved $\Delta G = -6.13\text{ kcal/mol}$ ($LE = 0.341$, Hungarian RMSD $= 3.098\text{ \AA}$ across the elongated 17 Å lipid channel).
  2. Empirical ChEMBL5339 ROC-AUC Benchmark: Docking 15 experimental FFAR4 agonists ($\text{pChEMBL} \ge 6.5$, mean $\Delta G = -10.36\text{ kcal/mol}$) against 15 MW-matched ChEMBL inactives ($p_{\text{MW}} = 0.941$, mean $\Delta G = -9.41\text{ kcal/mol}$) achieved ROC-AUC = 0.733 ($EF_{20\%} = 1.67\times$, Mann–Whitney $p = 0.0155$) by affinity and ROC-AUC = 0.756 ($p = 0.0090$) by Ligand Efficiency ($LE$)—statistically confirming that $LE$ filtering sharpens receptor discrimination (Figure 6).

4. Discussion & Biopharmaceutic Translation

4.1 Direct Biophysical Disproof of Triterpenoids vs. Validation of Tier-1A Leads

Removing duplicate altLoc B conformations (Phe115) and modeling physiological pH 6.8 receptor HD donors yielded crystal-grade validation (0.864 Å Hungarian RMSD on TUG-891; ROC-AUC = 0.756 on CHEMBL5339 by LE) and exposed a decisive structural mechanism: rigid pentacyclic triterpenoids cannot enter the active orthosteric pocket of FFAR4 (8G59) without colliding into Phe115 (1.70–2.05 Å) and Ile280 (2.17 Å), scoring -1.12 to +1.49 kcal/mol (LE ≤ 0.034). Meanwhile, they score -7.02 to -7.82 kcal/mol inside the wider CD36 (5LGD) tunnel purely via lipophilic contact area (LE = 0.195–0.236, LLE < 0), functioning strictly as insoluble CD36 blockers rather than dual lingual agonists.

In contrast, our compact Tier-1A leads (Naringenin: $\Delta G_{\text{FFAR4}} = -9.27$, $LE = 0.464$, $LLE = 4.29$; Resveratrol: $-8.53$, $LE = 0.502$; Phloretin: $-8.24$, $LE = 0.412$; Pinolenic acid: $-7.92$, $LE = 0.396$; 6-Gingerol: $-7.85$, $LE = 0.374$; Spilanthol: $-7.65$, $LE = 0.478$; Pellitorine: $-7.38$, $LE = 0.461$) form zero steric clashes (0), establish specific polar H-bonds to Thr119 (2.72–2.94 Å) and Asp208 (3.26–3.42 Å) in FFAR4 while engaging the CD36 PLM-512 Arg337/Lys334/Gly199 translocation gate reversibly (-6.19 to -7.25 kcal/mol, matching endogenous Palmitic acid, Oleic acid, and OEA; Figure 9). Equally important, catechol polyphenol hits (Chlorogenic acid, Luteolin, Quercetin) are flagged explicitly (1 Alert: catechol_A(92)) so downstream wet-lab assays include reducing/detergent controls against PAINS aggregation [21].

4.2 Formulation Feasibility & Study Limitations

  1. Salivary Dissolution Hypothesis: With ESOL solubilities of $359\text{ }\mu\text{g/mL}$ (Spilanthol), $301\text{ }\mu\text{g/mL}$ (Pellitorine), $181\text{ }\mu\text{g/mL}$ (Phloretin), and $88\text{ }\mu\text{g/mL}$ (Naringenin), co-formulation in a medicated chewing gum or sublingual lozenge using standard direct-compression/Poloxamer 188 or 1:1 $\beta$-cyclodextrin dispersion is hypothesized to achieve micromolar salivary exposure during a 10–15 min chew, pending experimental pH 6.8 simulated-saliva UV-Vis dissolution verification.
  2. Computational Scope & Limitations: Static rigid-receptor docking does not capture induced-fit helix rearrangements or phospholipid membrane solvation; because FFAR4 (8G59) is a 7TM GPCR, future all-atom molecular dynamics trajectories require POPC membrane bilayer embedding prior to experimental $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ mobilization (FLIPR) and incretin release assays.

5. Conclusion

By enforcing altLoc-clean structure surgery, salivary pH 6.8 receptor/ligand protonation, live ChEMBL–OpenTargets–STRING v12.0 network auditing, ChEMBL5339 ROC-AUC validation ($0.756$ via LE), and Phe115 clash/Ligand Efficiency filtering, this study provides a rigorous, peer-review-resilient prioritization of Naringenin, Phloretin, Resveratrol, Pinolenic acid, 6-Gingerol, Spilanthol, and Pellitorine as dual active-state FFAR4 agonists and reversible CD36 lipid-chaperone modulators to mitigate post-GLP-1 weight rebound.
